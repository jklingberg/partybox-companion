**Purpose.** Two symptoms dominate real use ahead of v1.0 and neither is
covered by the automatable suite: *"PartyBox doesn't show up in Spotify"* and
*"the music stutters"*, both reported as triggered by power-cycling the Pi or
by other Bluetooth devices being around. This session runs the VAL-PHYS
battery to attach a mechanism to each symptom, or to falsify the two
mechanisms hypothesised below.

---

## Hypotheses under test

Stated before the run so the result can falsify them.

### Mechanism A — "not in Spotify" is `audio_ready` with extra steps

librespot is the only process advertising Spotify Connect over Zeroconf, and
`_gate_spotify_on_audio` (`packages/companion/src/companion/__main__.py`) runs
it *only* while `audio_ready` is true plus `_AUDIO_GRACE_SECONDS` (300 s).

> **A1.** Any A2DP outage longer than 5 minutes removes PartyBox from every
> Spotify client's device list.
> **A2.** Recovery requires the full chain A2DP → librespot cold start →
> Zeroconf re-announce → client rediscovery, and the last link is client-side
> and outside our control.
> **A3.** After an *unclean* Pi power cut specifically, A2DP reconnection is
> additionally delayed by the speaker still holding a stale link to the
> vanished host, and librespot's Spotify login may fail against a
> pre-NTP `fake-hwclock` date.

### Mechanism B — "stutter" is the BLE reconnect loop stealing the radio

ADR-028 records a 2026-07-21 `btmon` capture proving the LE scan *start/stop
mode switch* on this BCM4345 combo controller costs a ~440 ms cluster of A2DP
TX gaps, independent of scan duration. `AudioFocusService` was gated to skip
scanning outright while streaming as a result. `DeviceManager`'s reconnect
loop was not: `_connect_and_maintain` calls `_scan()` unconditionally each
pass with a 5 s → 60 s backoff, and its `streaming_fn` only stretches the
*health-check* interval.

> **B1.** Whenever the BLE control link is down while A2DP audio plays, the
> appliance produces an audible click every 5 s ramping to every 60 s, for as
> long as the link stays down.
> **B2.** Anything that takes the speaker's single BLE central slot (the JBL
> app, another central) puts the appliance in exactly that state, which is why
> "other Bluetooth devices" correlates with stutter.
> **B3.** During an ADR-042 link-establishment storm (observed up to ~2 h 53 m)
> this would be continuous for the whole window.

**PHYS-08 is the decisive experiment for B**: the JBL app takes BLE while A2DP
stays up, so if the scan loop is innocent, audio is unaffected.

### Mechanism B′ — the healthy state may click once a minute

`_STREAMING_HEALTH_CHECK_INTERVAL` in `manager.py` exists because the *same*
2026-07-21 btmon session independently caught the health probe's GATT
round-trip as an audible click source, landing "exactly every ~15.2 s […]
with zero relation to any AudioFocusService activity". It was relaxed 15 s →
60 s while streaming. But the parallel FDDF finding on this controller was
that cadence cuts click *frequency* and never per-click *severity* — the
reason FDDF scanning was eventually gated off outright rather than slowed.

> **B′.** A fully healthy, fully connected, otherwise-idle appliance still
> emits an audible click roughly every 60 s while streaming, indefinitely.

If B′ holds, "the music stutters" may be partly a **steady-state** defect
present with no other Bluetooth device in the room, and every other stutter
observation this session must be read as sitting on top of that baseline.
PHYS-12 tests it first, before anything is perturbed.

## Results

> Format per the suite: Expected / Observed / Evidence / Follow-up.
> Filled in as each scenario runs.

### PHYS-12 — Steady-state click audit                    **FAIL**

- **Expected:** either silence (B′ false) or a click roughly every 60 s
  aligned with the GATT health probe (B′ true).
- **Observed:** neither. Operator reported *"the standard 30 s after start
  stuttering"* — several seconds of stutter shortly after playback began,
  which then stopped and did not recur. The operator's word "standard" is
  itself a finding: this is a familiar, repeatable event, not a one-off.
  The journal shows a cause entirely different from B′, and B′ is not
  supported — no periodic 60 s artefact appeared in the stable window.

- **Evidence** (`20260811T185725Z-phys-12-startup-stutter`):

  ```
  19:53:11.698  audio  A2DP connection established to 50:1B:6A:14:FD:1D
  19:53:11.699  __main__ audio gate: audio ready — starting Spotify Connect
  19:53:25.961  manager  battery recovered on re-probe: 100%
  19:54:12.037  manager  connection lost, will reconnect (72:60:29:A3:8A:D3)
  19:54:12.038  manager  scan attempt 2
  19:54:25.051  manager  scan attempt 3
  19:54:43.065  manager  scan attempt 4
  19:55:11.280  manager  scan attempt 5
  19:55:59.294  manager  scan attempt 6
  19:56:16.146  manager  connected to 7F:BD:B7:44:8F:01 (attempt 6)
  ```

  The BLE control link dropped **60.3 s after A2DP audio started flowing**,
  and the appliance then ran **six LE discovery scans over 2 m 04 s while
  music was playing** before reconnecting. Per ADR-028 each scan costs a
  ~440 ms cluster of A2DP TX gaps. The first three scans fall in a 31 s
  window, which matches the operator's "a few seconds of stuttering, then it
  went away"; the widening backoff (13 → 18 → 28 → 48 s) thins the clicks
  out until the reconnect ends them.

  This is **Mechanism B confirmed** — the audible artefact is
  `DeviceManager`'s reconnect scanning, which is not gated on streaming —
  but with a trigger not anticipated: the BLE link drop is *caused by
  playback starting*, so the stutter fires on essentially every listening
  session rather than only when a competing Bluetooth device shows up.

  Not a health-probe false positive: no `health probe failed (n/N)` line
  precedes the loss, so the drain task surfaced a platform-signalled
  disconnect (bleak's disconnected callback), not a probe timeout. The
  address change (`72:60:29:…` → `7F:BD:B7:…`) is ordinary RPA rotation.

  Post-reconnect the link stayed up for the remainder of the audit
  (≥ 3 m 34 s, `ble_connected: true`, `audio_ready: true`, music playing,
  no further scans), so the drop is a **one-shot disruption at stream
  start**, not a 60 s cycle.

- **Follow-up:** [OBS-1](#obs-1--adr-039-adapter-recovery-kills-the-audio-it-is-meant-to-protect)
  — the same root event has a heavy tail that produces the *other* reported
  symptom. Two fixes proposed under "Verdict".

## Observations (OBS-n)

### OBS-1 — ADR-039 adapter recovery kills the audio it is meant to protect

Found by grepping the retained journal for prior occurrences of the PHYS-12
signature. On **2026-08-09** the same start-of-playback BLE drop occurred, but
the reconnect fell into the long tail and cascaded into total audio loss —
this is the **"PartyBox disappeared from Spotify"** report, with a mechanism:

```
16:39:51  manager  connected to 7B:2F:33:7D:EC:81 (attempt 218)
16:40:34  manager  connection lost, will reconnect          ← 43 s after connect, audio playing
          … 25 LE scans over the next 11 minutes, music playing throughout …
16:51:34  manager  requesting Bluetooth adapter recovery: 652s without a
                   successful connect (ADR-039)
16:51:35  adapter_recovery  adapter reset: hci0 power-cycled  ← destroys A2DP too
16:51:45  audio    A2DP sink not connected, connecting to 50:1B:6A:14:FD:1D
16:51:45  __main__ audio gate: audio unavailable — 300s grace before stopping Spotify
16:51:46  audio    A2DP connect failed: err:'br-connection-unknown'
   …      (five consecutive failures)
16:54:12  audio    5 consecutive outright connect failures — cooling down 300s
16:56:45  __main__ audio gate: grace period expired — stopping Spotify Connect
17:02:03  asyncio  ERROR Future exception was never retrieved
                   BrokenPipeError(32, 'Broken pipe')  (bleak bluezdbus client.connect)
17:06:59  manager  connected to 51:0F:1E:9A:0E:C8 (attempt 244)
```

**Both reported symptoms are the same bug.** One root event — playback start
knocks out the BLE control link — with two outcomes depending on how fast the
reconnect lands:

- *Common tail (~2 min):* a few seconds of stutter from the scan loop, then
  it settles. What the operator calls "standard".
- *Heavy tail (>652 s):* ADR-039's watchdog fires and power-cycles `hci0`.
  That adapter reset **tears down the A2DP link as collateral damage** —
  `AudioService` notices exactly 10 s later — and the speaker then refuses
  reconnection with `br-connection-unknown` for long enough to burn the five
  retries, the 300 s cooldown, *and* the 300 s Spotify grace. librespot is
  killed, the Zeroconf advertisement is withdrawn, and PartyBox vanishes
  from every Spotify client. Total outage: **26 minutes**, self-inflicted.

The self-healing mechanism is what breaks the audio. ADR-039's recovery has no
awareness that A2DP audio is streaming, and power-cycling the controller while
it carries an audio stream cannot not break it.

Contributing: the 652 s escalation threshold is measured from the last
*successful connect*, which during playback is a state the appliance may
legitimately be unable to reach — see the Verdict.

### OBS-2 — Unhandled `BrokenPipeError` traceback from bleak

`Future exception was never retrieved` / `BrokenPipeError(32, 'Broken pipe')`
out of `bleak.backends.bluezdbus.client.connect` at 17:02:03, mid-storm. Not
obviously harmful, but it is an unretrieved-future ERROR with a raw traceback
in a consumer appliance's log, during the exact window an operator would be
reading it. VAL-LOG treats that as a defect.

## Verdict on the hypotheses

| | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **A** — "not in Spotify" is `audio_ready` + 300 s grace | **Confirmed**, but it is a *consequence*, not the root cause. The grace expiry at 16:56:45 on Aug 09 behaved exactly as designed; what put `audio_ready` false was OBS-1's adapter reset. Fixing the gate would only mask it. |
| **A3** — unclean-cut-specific delays (stale speaker link, pre-NTP clock) | **Untested.** PHYS-01/02 not yet run. Still worth running, but demoted: the observed Spotify disappearance needed no power cut at all. |
| **B** — stutter is the ungated reconnect scan loop | **Confirmed.** Six scans in 2 m 04 s with audio playing, matching the operator's report. Trigger is worse than hypothesised: the BLE drop is caused by playback starting, so this fires on ordinary listening sessions, not just when a competing device appears. |
| **B′** — healthy state clicks every 60 s | **Not supported.** ≥ 3 m 34 s of stable, connected playback with no reported artefact and no correlating log activity. Worth one longer confirmation run, but the 15 s → 60 s relaxation appears to have been sufficient. |

### Proposed fixes (both small, both have precedent in-tree)

1. **Gate `DeviceManager._scan()` on streaming.** `AudioFocusService` already
   skips scanning outright while audio flows, for exactly this reason and on
   exactly this evidence (ADR-028). The reconnect loop was never given the
   same treatment. Removes the audible stutter in the common tail, and stops
   the appliance fighting the radio it is trying to stream over. Implies
   accepting "BLE control down while audio streams" as a *normal, expected*
   state rather than a fault to recover from — which is what the hardware
   appears to be telling us it is.
2. **Never power-cycle `hci0` while A2DP audio is streaming.** ADR-039's
   watchdog currently has no streaming awareness, and its recovery action is
   strictly destructive to an active stream. Gating it removes the 26-minute
   self-inflicted outage entirely. If the adapter is genuinely wedged the
   reset can wait for silence; the user is, by definition, still hearing
   music.

Fix 1 also largely prevents fix 2's trigger: with no scanning during
playback, the "652 s without a successful connect" clock stops being driven
by a condition playback itself creates.

### Consequences for the rest of the battery

PHYS-06/07/08 (competing Bluetooth devices) should still run, but the
hypothesis has changed: contention is no longer needed to explain the
stutter, so those scenarios now test whether a competing device *worsens*
an artefact that is already present, and whether it pushes reconnect into
the heavy tail where OBS-1's cascade fires.
